## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment: authored, but only one component deep.**

The product card is genuinely distinctive — a #D3A067 plank with a lighter #E0C195 lit top edge at elevation 4 on a cool #F5FAFC wall. In a category of white cards with chevrons, this list is recognizable in a thumbnail. Manual reorder as the primary IA and duplicate as a first-class card action are both considered, domain-aware choices.

Everything else is stock Material with a color swap. Settings is two bare ListTiles. Tags is a TextField over a ListView. Add Product is an unmodified AlertDialog. Remove the seven constants in colors.dart and four of five surfaces revert to a Flutter starter app.

The deeper miss is that the metaphor is applied literally rather than structurally. The launcher icon is three neutral wooden shelves holding twenty saturated jars — the jars are the subject, the shelves are the armature. The app inverted it: shelf brown became the largest, loudest surface, and the jars appear nowhere except user-authored tag chips parked at the bottom of the card. Structurally these aren't shelves either — Card's 4dp margin and 12dp radius make each row a detached floating plank. Thirty products is thirty identical planks: a stack of lumber, not a shelf.

**Deterministic scan:** detect.mjs ran clean on lib/ and on product_card.dart — exit 0, empty array, both times. The detector has only web-markup rules and no Dart coverage, so this is "not applicable," not a clean bill of health. Zero signal either way.

**Rendered evidence** (11 PNGs in /tmp/shelflife_shots/, 360x780 logical at dpr 3): no browser exists for an Android app, so the UI was rendered directly through flutter_test with real fonts loaded. This substituted for browser overlays.

## Overall Impression

The card is the best thing here and it is carrying the entire product alone. It looks like an object, the warm palette clears AAA contrast on its largest surface, and the type discipline is exemplary — not one hardcoded fontSize in the codebase, so system font scaling propagates perfectly.

And then the app cannot say anything about a product. There is no visual state vocabulary at all. A product that is fine, due next week, and eight months overdue render identically: same brown, same header, same position, same 14sp regular type. Overdue prints as a negative integer mid-stack.

The single biggest opportunity is not a restyle. It is that the app's one job — tell me what to replace — has no visual expression, and the space to give it one already exists on every card, currently occupied by a meaningless balance-scale glyph.

## What's Working

**The card as a physical object.** Elevation 4 (4x Material's default) plus a #E0C195 header strip clipped by the card's own 12dp radius makes a top edge that reads as *lit* rather than as a header band. That converts a data row into an object, which converts a list into a shelf. It is also why the app is recognizable at Play Store thumbnail size — a real commercial asset.

**Warm surfaces that don't cost legibility.** #2B1719 on #D3A067 measures 7.26:1; on #E0C195, 9.87:1; on #C4D9A0, 11.12:1. Colored-surface apps almost always pay for warmth with 3.5:1 body text. Choosing a red-cast near-black over the generated #171C1E is a refined call most teams would never make.

**Total type discipline.** Every style resolves from a Material role, so the user's system font-scale setting works end to end. At 1.5x the product list degrades gracefully — cards grow, text wraps to two and three lines, nothing clips. Most hand-styled apps forfeit this on day one. Any hierarchy fix must come from role changes, never hardcoded sizes.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The app has no state vocabulary, so it cannot perform its own core function

**Why it matters:** Nothing in the product surface changes when an item is due, overdue, or fine. A notification fires, the user opens the app, and the screen is byte-identical to yesterday's — with no indication of which of thirty cards it meant. The notification copy says "open the app to reset," and no control named reset exists. Overdue renders as "Months to Replacement: -2", an unstyled negative number in the middle of a flat three-line stack. This is the product's one job, unimplemented at the visual layer, and it lands at the end of the core loop where peak-end weights it double.

**Fix:** One new state axis, three steps, using a new dedicated color rather than recruiting JAR_RED or JAR_GREEN. (a) Due and overdue cards get a 4dp left edge bar plus a saturated header tint step. (b) The months figure becomes the card's largest element, switching role from bodyMedium to headlineSmall when at or under one month. (c) Overdue prints a word, not a negative integer. (d) The Icons.scale glyph — currently carrying zero information in a permanent 40dp left-aligned slot on every row, the highest-value real estate in a scan — becomes the state glyph. That one costs no space at all.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable colorize, then /impeccable layout

### [P0] The empty state does not exist

**Why it matters:** Measured: 98.37% of the body area on first launch is a single flat #F5FAFC. The only pixels below the app bar are the FAB. No illustration, no heading, no instruction — the app's own name "Shelf Life" appears nowhere on screen, only in the OS task switcher. This is the first frame of every user's relationship with the app and the frame Play Store screenshots capture. A void reads as broken, not minimal. It is also the only chance to teach the two concepts the app depends on — what "months to replacement" counts from, and that tags are user-created — and it forfeits both.

**Fix:** Repeat the icon's own composition: one drawn shelf line with two or three colored jars, "Shelf Life" at headlineSmall, one line of body copy, and a filled high-contrast primary button — not the ghostly FAB — reading "Add your first product." Separate copy for the empty-after-filter case with a one-tap clear.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable onboard

### [P1] The card has no internal hierarchy, and the copy reads as a database dump

**Why it matters:** Get Again, Months to Replacement, and Cost render at identical size, weight, color, and indent. Every line is prefixed with its field name, pushing the only variable part — the value — 21 to 24 characters right, which is statistically the least-fixated position in a left-to-right scan. Scanning a list means sampling one anchor per row; this card offers none. "Get Again: Yes" additionally takes the top slot, the highest visual priority, for the least consequential fact.

**Fix:** Invert the stack and diversify roles. Lead with the figure ("3 months left" / "Overdue") at headlineSmall. Demote cost to a right-aligned secondary. Replace "Get Again: Yes/No" with a presence signal — a small repeat glyph when true, absent when false. Silence is the correct rendering of "No", and it deletes a line from every card.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable typeset, then /impeccable clarify

### [P1] Tag chips are unreadable on 7 of 21 user-selectable colors

**Why it matters:** product_card.dart:89 hardcodes chip labels to #2B1719 regardless of the chosen background. Against BlockPicker's default palette: black #000000 gives 1.24:1, Deep Purple 2.31:1, Indigo 2.47:1, Brown 2.59:1, Purple 2.69:1. Five swatches fall below 3:1. Rendered proof: on a chip filled #2B1719 the label area contains exactly one unique color — the text has zero distinguishable pixels. It is a blank rounded rectangle. The same fixed foreground is used on TagsPage tiles, where a dark tag makes the row title 1.01:1 and the leading palette icon 1.00:1 — both literally invisible. DESIGN.md's own Borrowed Color Rule ("never assume contrast against one") is violated by the app that generated it.

**Fix:** Choose the label color per swatch by background luminance. Computed across the full palette, dynamic black/white yields a worst case of 4.80:1 — every swatch passes AA. Apply to chips, TagsPage tiles, and both tag icons. Also drop the chip's BorderSide(color: SHELF_BROWN): it draws the border in the exact card color, 1.00:1, so it is a no-op everywhere except on dark chips.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable colorize

### [P2] Destructive actions are 24dp, unlabeled, unconfirmed, and duplicated

**Why it matters:** Measured at 24x24dp — half Android's 48dp minimum — as bare GestureDetectors with no ripple, no tooltip, no semanticLabel. Delete is the outermost square at right:8, the most accident-prone position in the layout, in the hardest region for a one-handed right thumb. On top of that the whole card is a Dismissible that deletes in either direction with no background widget, so it slides off over bare scaffold with no red, no trash glyph, no preview of consequence. Every row is simultaneously a two-way delete gesture and a reorder drag handle. Nothing confirms, nothing undoes, and there is no cloud copy — data loss is permanent.

**Fix:** Keep the 24dp visual, wrap to a 48dp hit area (this preserves DESIGN.md's Right-Angle Exception). Give the Dismissible a reveal background in error #BA1A1A with a trash glyph, one direction only. Emit an undo snackbar on every delete. Move delete out of the corner cluster — three peers where one is irreversible is wrong grouping, and removing it shrinks the cluster from 96dp to 64dp.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable harden

### [P2] The primary action is the lowest-contrast object in the app

**Why it matters:** The FAB fill #AEECFF sits at 1.23:1 against the #F5FAFC scaffold — visible only by its shadow — and 1.29:1 against the #99D1E2 app bar it was seeded from, so the two blues read as a near-miss rather than a pair. On the empty screen it is the only actionable thing and it barely renders. Separately, the filter icon is visually identical whether zero or five tags are active, so a user who filtered last session opens the app, sees three of thirty products, and concludes the app lost their data.

**Fix:** Set the FAB explicitly to primary #00687B with a white icon. That also resolves DESIGN.md's recorded tension that the scheme's most saturated color never appears as a fill anywhere. Badge the filter icon with an active count and render a dismissible chip row beneath the app bar showing which tags are filtering.

**Suggested command:** /impeccable polish

## Persona Red Flags

**Sam (screen reader / low vision):** The three card actions are GestureDetector + Icon with no semanticLabel and no Semantics wrapper — TalkBack announces nothing, so Sam cannot delete, edit, or duplicate any product at all. Icons.scale leads every row unlabeled and meaningless. Non-text contrast fails twice: the #C4D9A0 squares against the #D3A067 card measure 1.53:1 against a 3:1 requirement, so the controls do not separate from their container; the #E0C195 header against the #D3A067 body is 1.36:1, so the card's primary structural division is essentially invisible. Dark tag chips are blank rectangles. One genuine credit: no hardcoded fontSize anywhere means Sam's system font scale works throughout, and the 1.5x render confirms it holds.

**Casey (distracted, one-handed, on the move):** The three most-used controls are pinned to the top-right of every card — the hardest one-handed reach and the region most likely to be brushed while scrolling. Delete is the outermost of them. A slightly horizontal scroll flick deletes a product with no confirmation and no undo. Nothing is scannable, which is exactly what Casey needs standing in a shop aisle: answering "do I need conditioner?" means reading line two of three, at the end of a 21-character label, on the right one of thirty identical brown cards. The FAB at 1.23:1 is hard to find outdoors. And the list has no bottom padding — the render shows the FAB sitting directly on top of the last card's header.

**Jordan (first-timer):** Opens to a blank white screen and a pale disc; first decision is whether the app installed correctly. Neither core concept is ever explained — "Months to Replacement" from when? No date appears anywhere in the app, so there is nothing to infer the baseline from. The dialog is titled "Add New Product" even when editing, contradicted by its own Save button. The three green squares have no ripple, no rounding, no shadow, and 1.53:1 separation, so Jordan may not read them as buttons at all.

## Minor Observations

- **The 1.5x dialog overflows.** Confirmed in the render: a yellow-and-black hazard box at the Months to Replacement row, the label sliced mid-word to "Replacemen", and the input's underline pushed outside the dialog's own surface. Root cause is a hard SizedBox(width: 50) against an unconstrained label in a spaceBetween Row (add_product_dialog.dart:146).
- **The tag chip row clips mid-glyph with no affordance.** Rendered: chips cut at exactly the 16dp padding boundary, "bathroom" sliced through the second o, and 3 of 7 tags entirely invisible. No fade, no scrollbar, no "+3" counter. A tag whose name happens to end at the boundary would give no hint at all.
- **French breaks the dialog, not the list.** The product list in French is near pixel-identical to English — card copy is short and has abundant slack. But the dialog's OverflowBar breaks into a three-row vertical stack (Annuler / Modifier les étiquettes / Ajouter) with the primary action at the bottom.
- **A minimal product looks broken.** No price, no months, no tags collapses to a header plus the single line "Get Again: No" — a thin brown slab containing one piece of schema.
- **Card heights swing wildly** — 133dp to 275dp in the same render — purely on which optional fields exist, while the action squares stay pinned at a fixed y=8, so they float at a different relative position on every card.
- **The app's own name never appears in the UI.** The one branding surface is spent on "Your Products".
- **The 28dp top pad creates a dead band** across the top-left 60% of every card; the squares only occupy the right 96dp, so the left side gains clearance it doesn't need.
- **Settings shows "21:00" to a 12-hour-locale user** — the row bypasses MaterialLocalizations.formatTimeOfDay while the picker it opens is locale-aware, so the two disagree.
- **Settings uses two different affordances for the same interaction** — plain trailing Text for time, filled ElevatedButton for currency, whose entire label is a single "$" glyph.
- **The dialog's number rows are visibly ragged** — Price and Months underlines have different widths and different left edges, and the "$" floats detached outside its field.
- **No motion design anywhere.** In an app whose premise is the passage of time, nothing ever moves.

## Questions to Consider

1. Your launcher icon is twenty colored jars on three neutral shelves. Your app is thirty identical brown shelves and no jars. Which one is the product? What happens if the shelf becomes the list's continuous structure and each product becomes a jar whose fill encodes time remaining?
2. If you deleted the Cost line from every card, would one user complain? It holds a permanent third of the card body for a number nobody consults while scanning.
3. The notification says "open the app to reset." Where is reset? Why doesn't the app open onto the product it woke you about?
4. You wrote a No-State-Color Rule and made it a principle. Is it a principle, or a description of a feature that hasn't been built? The app's only reason to exist is state.
5. Icons.scale appears on every card and means nothing. You have a free, permanent, 40dp, left-aligned slot on every row — the highest-value real estate in a scan. What should be there?
6. Your FAB is the lowest-contrast object in the app at 1.23:1, and your theme's most saturated color #00687B is never used as a fill anywhere. Is there a reason to keep both facts true at once?
